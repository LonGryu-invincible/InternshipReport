# Event 1 — AWS Cloud and AI Day Hanoi: Ho Chi Minh City Watch Party

## 1. Event Overview

- **Date:** September 29, 2026
- **Location:** 26th Floor, Bitexco Financial Tower, District 1, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam
- **Format:** Watch party
- **Role:** Attendee

AWS Cloud and AI Day is an event organized by Amazon Web Services (AWS), focusing on trends and practical applications of cloud computing and artificial intelligence in business environments.

I attended the event through a watch party at the AWS office in Ho Chi Minh City, where participants followed presentations from the main event in Hanoi. As a student interested in machine learning, I was particularly interested in Generative AI, Agentic AI, and the challenges of building AI systems that can operate in real-world environments.

## 2. Key Highlights

### 2.1. Cloud and AI in Business

One point that stood out to me was how businesses choose technologies based on the problems they need to solve. Previously, when exploring a new technology, I usually focused on its features and technical capabilities. The event helped me understand that a solution must also fit an organization's workflows, resources, and budget.

This is especially relevant to AI: a well-performing model is only one part of a product. Bringing a model to users also requires appropriate data, infrastructure, and operational processes.

### 2.2. Agentic AI — From Demonstrations to Real-World Applications

Agentic AI was one of the topics I found most interesting. Unlike a chatbot that mainly answers questions, an AI agent can be designed to use tools, retrieve information, and coordinate multiple steps to complete tasks.

From the event topics and related materials, I identified several important considerations for deploying agents:

- **Reliability:** Evaluate agents across different situations, not only prepared examples.
- **Access Control:** Restrict access to the tools and resources agents actually need.
- **Observability:** Monitor agent activities to detect and investigate failures.
- **Evaluation:** Assess quality using clear criteria and test scenarios.
- **Human Oversight:** Maintain human review of important outputs and decisions.

I realized that producing good answers is not enough to ensure that an AI agent can operate reliably in a real-world environment.

### 2.3. Data Foundations for AI

The event also highlighted the importance of data in AI development. Some concepts I explored further included:

- **Data Lake:** A platform for storing data from different sources, potentially using Amazon S3.
- **Data Governance:** Managing data access and usage.
- **Data Quality:** Ensuring that data is complete, accurate, and consistent.
- **Data Traceability:** Tracking data sources and processing steps.

As a machine learning student, I already understood that data quality affects model performance. However, this topic helped me see the broader picture: data must be managed not only before training but also throughout the operation of an AI system.

### 2.4. Application Modernization

Another topic was application modernization, including AI-assisted solutions such as AWS Transform. I learned that modernization is not simply about replacing old technology with new technology. Cost, scalability, compatibility, and operational complexity also need to be considered.

## 3. Personal Experience

For me, AWS Cloud and AI Day was an opportunity to see cloud and AI concepts discussed in a business context. Before attending, I mainly approached AI through models, algorithms, and evaluation metrics. The event encouraged me to pay greater attention to data quality, system controls, and deployment requirements.

It was also an opportunity to experience a professional technology event at the AWS office instead of learning only through documentation and hands-on exercises.

## 4. Lessons Learned

**First, data quality matters throughout the AI system lifecycle.** Even a well-performing model can produce unreliable results if its input data is not properly managed.

**Second, evaluating a model is not the same as evaluating an entire system.** In addition to accuracy, reliability, security, and operational requirements must be considered.

**Third, technology choices should reflect actual needs.** A simple architecture that meets the requirements may be more appropriate than a complex, resource-intensive solution.

These lessons encouraged me to continue developing my knowledge of both machine learning and cloud computing.
