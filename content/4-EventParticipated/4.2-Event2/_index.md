# Event 2 — Smart Plushies for Children

## 1. Event Overview

- **Date:** October 26, 2026
- **Location:** 36th Floor, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam
- **Format:** Volunteering activity
- **Role:** Volunteer

Smart Plushies for Children was a volunteering activity organized within the AWS community in Ho Chi Minh City. Its goal was to complete interactive stuffed animals that could produce sounds and would be given to children.

Unlike the technology events I had attended before, this activity did not focus on technical presentations. Instead, participants worked directly on completing and testing products for a community-oriented purpose.

## 2. Main Activities

### 2.1. Completing the Stuffed Animals

During the event, I worked with one other participant in a two-person team. All materials were prepared by the organizers, including bear covers with zippers, stuffing, and electronic circuits for the interactive functions.

Our responsibility was to complete the toys using the provided materials. We did not sew the covers or design, program, or manufacture the circuits. The process involved:

1. Receiving the materials provided by the organizers.
2. Opening the zippers and placing stuffing inside the bear covers.
3. Adjusting the stuffing to give the toys a balanced shape.
4. Closing the zippers and inspecting the completed toys.

At first, I thought stuffing the toys would be straightforward. However, while doing the task, I realized that the stuffing needed to be distributed carefully to maintain the intended shape. Since there were only two of us, we could coordinate directly throughout each step.

### 2.2. Testing the Electronic Circuits

The part that interested me most was testing the electronic circuits inside the stuffed animals. The circuits were designed to produce sounds in response to certain physical interactions. We tested two actions:

- **Gently stroking the bear's head:** Checking whether touching the head triggered a sound.
- **Holding and gently shaking the bear:** Checking whether movement activated an audible response.

These actions helped us check whether the circuits responded as expected. I did not design or program the circuits, but testing them gave me a practical introduction to **functional testing** through a physical product.

A stuffed animal might look complete, but its sound functions still need to be checked before it is handed over.

### 2.3. Purpose of the Activity

According to the organizers, the completed toys were intended as gifts for children and were associated with STEM learning cards. Combining a traditional stuffed toy with interactive functions added a technical element to the volunteering activity.

## 3. Personal Experience

This was a different kind of experience during my participation in FCAJ. Instead of learning about an AWS service or attending a technical presentation, I was directly involved in completing a product and testing its functionality.

What made the activity meaningful to me was knowing that the finished products were intended for a community initiative. Although the tasks were not particularly complex, they still required care before handover. Working with another participant also gave me an opportunity to practice teamwork in a small group where both members contributed to the final result.

## 4. Lessons Learned

**From a technical perspective**, I learned that testing should reflect how people actually interact with a product. Stroking or gently shaking the stuffed animals provided simple but useful ways to verify their basic electronic functions.

**In terms of teamwork**, the activity showed me that coordination and mutual support matter even when a team has only two members.

**Regarding responsibility**, I realized that completing a task involves more than finishing the assigned steps. It also means checking the result.

Finally, the event showed me another side of the technology community: beyond developing technical solutions, people can also create value through volunteering and community activities.
